# Business-Date Windows (no SYSDATE)

**Date:** 2026-10-16

## 1. What and why

Scenario SQL from the PioTech service used Oracle's clock: `TRA_DATE >= SYSDATE - 30`. That made every rule depend on
the moment it ran, made shadow tests look at "today" (0 alerts on historical data), and forced the alert engine to
patch the SQL text for past dates.

Now no scenario SQL reads the clock. Windows are bound to an explicit **business date** and counted backward across
**business days, Sunday–Thursday** (Friday and Saturday skipped).

## 2. How a window is counted

A window of N business days covers the business date plus the N−1 business days before it. Transactions dated on a
weekend day inside that span still count; only the counting skips weekends.

| Business date | Window | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Sun 30 Jun 2019 | 1 business day | Sun 30 Jun |
| Sun 30 Jun 2019 | 2 business days | Thu 27 Jun – Sun 30 Jun |
| Sun 30 Jun 2019 | 7 business days | Thu 20 Jun – Sun 30 Jun |
| Sat 29 Jun 2019 | 2 business days | Wed 26 Jun – Sat 29 Jun (counting starts Thursday) |

Weeks count 5 business days each. Months and years stay calendar (`ADD_MONTHS`). Calendar periods ("this month")
use `TRUNC(:as_of_date, 'MM')`.

## 3. How the SQL is rewritten

| Generated by PioTech | Executed and saved as |
|---|---|
| `TRA_DATE >= SYSDATE - 30` | `TRA_DATE >= :bd_minus_30 AND TRA_DATE < :as_of_date + 1` |
| `TRA_DATE >= TRUNC(SYSDATE) - 1` | `TRA_DATE >= :bd_minus_1 AND TRA_DATE < :as_of_date + 1` |
| `TRA_DATE >= ADD_MONTHS(SYSDATE, -1)` | `TRA_DATE >= ADD_MONTHS(:as_of_date, -1) AND TRA_DATE < :as_of_date + 1` |
| `TRA_DATE >= TRUNC(SYSDATE, 'MM')` | `TRA_DATE >= TRUNC(:as_of_date, 'MM') AND TRA_DATE < :as_of_date + 1` |
| baseline `>= SYSDATE - 210 AND < SYSDATE - 30` | `>= :bd_minus_210 AND < :bd_minus_30` |

`:as_of_date` is the business date; `:bd_minus_N` is the first day of the N-business-day window ending at it. Both are
computed in Python (`business_dates.window_start`) and passed as bind variables.

## 4. Which business date is used

| Where | Business date |
|---|---|
| Shadow test | The scenario's `anchor_date` → `SHADOW_ANCHOR_DATE` setting → latest `TRA_DATE` in `PIO_TRANSACTIONS` for the configured country/institution → today |
| Alert engine | Its evaluation date (`run_alert_engine.py --date`, or `evaluation_date` on `POST /engine/run-scenarios`); defaults to the run date |

Rules saved before this change still contain `SYSDATE`; the alert engine rewrites them the same way when it runs them.
Nothing in the database is modified.

### 4a. Explicit fixed periods (added 2026-10-17)

A request that names a calendar period ("between 2016 and 2022", "in 2019", "from March 2020 to June 2021",
"2020-01-01 to 2020-03-31", "2016-2022") is evaluated on that period, not on a window counted back from today.

| Step | What happens | Example: *"cash withdrawal above 50,000 between 2016 and 2022"* |
|---|---|---|
| Intent (`analyze_intent_…`) | `anchor_date` = last day of the period; `time_window` = its exact length, `is_rolling: false` (whole years → YEARS, whole months → MONTHS, else DAYS) | `anchor_date: 2022-12-31`, `7 YEARS` |
| Text sent to PioTech | Period wording is removed from `detection_logic`, and condition phrases that state a period are dropped, so the years are never read as amounts. The period travels only in `anchor_date` and `time_window` | `"... above 50,000"` |
| SQL | After the business-date rewrite, the single window start becomes `DATE 'start'` and the evaluation date `DATE 'end'` | `TRA_DATE >= DATE '2016-01-01' AND TRA_DATE < DATE '2023-01-01'` |
| Shadow result | `evaluation_date` = period end, plus `fixed_period: {start, end, pinned_in_sql}` | `2022-12-31` |
| Plan | Evaluation Mode: *Fixed period `2016-01-01` to `2022-12-31`* | |

Numbers are only read as years in 1950–2099, and never when they carry a separator, `$`, a currency or `%`
("between 8,000 and 9,999", "2000 JOD" and "more than 2020 transactions" are amounts or counts).

A stated window much shorter than the period ("more than 10,000 JOD **within 7 days**, from 2020 into 2022") is the
rule's own window and is kept. The period then sets only the evaluation date (31 Dec 2022) and is removed from the
text; the SQL is not widened to the whole period (`pinned_in_sql: false`).

## 5. Files

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `web/services/business_dates.py` | Business-day calendar and window arithmetic; `find_fixed_period`, `window_for_period`, `period_of`, `apply_fixed_period`, `clean_for_sql_service` |
| `web/services/sql_dates.py` | `anchor_sql()` rewrite, `bind_values()`, `pin_period()` |

## 6. How to run and verify

```powershell
$env:PYTHONPATH = "services\aml_builder"
pytest services\aml_builder\tests\test_business_dates.py -q
```

Live: set `SHADOW_ANCHOR_DATE=2019-06-30` in `.env`, restart, run a scenario to the shadow test. The result shows
`evaluation_date`, `window_dates` and SQL with `:as_of_date` / `:bd_minus_N` instead of `SYSDATE`.

Fixed period: ask *"Flag any single cash withdrawal above 50,000 between 2016 and 2022"* and approve the plan. The
plan shows *Fixed period `2016-01-01` to `2022-12-31`*; the shadow result shows `evaluation_date: 2022-12-31` and SQL
bounded by `DATE '2016-01-01'` and `DATE '2023-01-01'` (verified on OPENBI2, 2026-10-17: 65 customers).

## 7. Configuration

| Variable | Default | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `BUSINESS_WEEKEND_DAYS` | `FRI,SAT` | Days skipped when counting windows |
| `SHADOW_ANCHOR_DATE` | unset | Business date for shadow tests when the scenario has no `anchor_date` |

## 8. Not covered

- Gaps between two events inside a sequence rule ("then within 1 day", written by PioTech as `in_date + 1`) are still
  calendar days.
- Public holidays are not in the calendar; only weekends are skipped.
- ~~A short window inside a period is evaluated once, for the window ending on the period's last day.~~ Since
  2026-10-19 it **slides across the whole period** (`sql_dates.slide_window_over_period`) for rules grouped per customer
  / account with a HAVING condition ("3 deposits in one week, in 2019" → any week of 2019). Still evaluated once: rules
  with DISTINCT counts, "fewer than" conditions or ORDER BY, and per-transaction rules.
- When the SQL has several different window starts (e.g. a baseline window), the period is not pinned; the windows
  are counted back from the period's last day.
